# Loomora reporting conventions

House formats for the recurring asks. Reuse the structures so consultants get a
familiar shape every month and can compare one report against the last.

## Audience rules

The readers are business consultants working for Loomora's commercial teams.
They are fluent in retail commercially and not at all technically.

- Never show SQL, column names, file names, table names or tool output in a
  report. Translate everything into business language.
- Name a metric, then give the number, then say what it means. `days_of_cover`
  becomes "about six months of stock on hand at the current sales rate".
- Round for reading: units to the nearest hundred above 10,000, money to the
  nearest thousand above EUR 100k. Keep percentages to one decimal.
- Always pair a number with its comparison, either the prior period or the
  marketplace average for the same month. A number on its own is not a finding.
- Lead with what changed and what to do about it, not with the data inventory.

## Monthly performance overview

The default deliverable. One provider or the whole marketplace, one month
against the month before and the same month a year earlier where the data
reaches back that far.

1. **Headline**: two or three sentences. What happened, why it matters, the one
   thing that needs a decision.
2. **Commercial performance**: units, net revenue, commission, contribution and
   contribution margin, each against the prior month and the year-ago month.
3. **Returns**: rate, direction, and the reason codes that moved. Name the
   product lines carrying the problem, not just the provider total.
4. **Availability**: stock-outs and days of cover, with the SKUs at either
   extreme called out and lost sales estimated where stock-outs occurred.
5. **Supply reliability**: OTIF, delay when late, goods-in rejects and ASN errors.
6. **Customer sentiment**: weighted average rating and direction.
7. **Watch list**: three to five specific items with an owner implied.

For a marketplace-wide overview, replace sections 2 through 6 with a provider
league table sorted by contribution, then a short "movers" section covering the
three biggest risers and the three biggest fallers by contribution.

## Procurement discussion prep

For a consultant walking into a negotiation or a review with a provider.
Framed for the meeting, not for the archive.

1. **Where we stand**: the relationship in four or five lines. Tier, time on
   the marketplace, share of category, contract renewal date, trajectory.
2. **What is working**: the genuine strengths, stated plainly. A negotiation
   opens better when the provider hears their wins first, and consultants need
   these to trade against.
3. **What needs to change**: at most three issues, each with the number, the
   business impact in euros or units, and the specific behaviour being asked for.
   Not "improve quality" but "add half-size guidance to the Halden size chart
   and re-grade the waistband".
4. **The ask**: what a good outcome looks like, with a fallback position.
   Where a commercial lever is involved (commission, terms, marketing support,
   assortment slots), state the lever and leave the number to the consultant.
5. **Expect them to say**: two or three counter-arguments the provider will
   reasonably make, each with the counter drawn from the data.
6. **Questions to ask**: things the data cannot answer that only the provider knows.

Estimate impact wherever possible. "Return rate rose 3.4 points" persuades
nobody; "the increase costs about EUR 41,000 a year in handling alone, before
the lost margin" starts a negotiation.

## Product deep dive

For "why is this line underperforming". Sales trend, the funnel from views to
cart to order, return rate against the provider and marketplace average, the
reason split, stock availability through the period, and rating trend. Then a
short read on which of the four usual causes fits the evidence: the product,
the price, the presentation, or the availability.

## Handling limits honestly

The data covers what it covers. When an ask runs past it, say so in one line and
offer the nearest available cut rather than reaching for a plausible number.

- No cost of goods, so gross margin on the product itself cannot be calculated.
  Commission and contribution are the available profitability measures.
- No customer-level or basket-level data, so repeat purchase, cohort behaviour
  and cross-sell are out of reach.
- No competitor or market-share data, so "versus the market" means versus the
  other providers on this marketplace.
- No marketing spend, so return on ad spend and channel attribution are out of
  reach. Traffic is visible, its cause is not.
- The return handling cost is a planning assumption. Flag it as one whenever it
  carries a recommendation.

Never fill one of these gaps with an invented figure. A named gap is useful to a
consultant; a fabricated number is a liability in a supplier meeting.
